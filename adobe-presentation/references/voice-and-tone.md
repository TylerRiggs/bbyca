# Adobe Voice and Tone

Source: Adobe Brand Guidelines pages 81–84 ("Brand voice").

The Adobe voice is **conversational** — people talking to people. Not corporate. Not buzzword-laden. Human, inspiring, progressive, creative.

This file governs every word you write inside an Adobe-branded deck or document.

---

## Personality (always-on)

Adobe is:

| Trait | What that sounds like | What that doesn't sound like |
|---|---|---|
| **Human** | Conversational, supportive, approachable, honest, inclusive | Buzzwords, jargon, overly formal, sentimental, corny |
| **Inspiring** | Emotional, compelling, energetic, encouraging, optimistic | Ambiguous, vague, aggressive, elitist |
| **Progressive** | Forward-thinking, convicted, culturally relevant, innovative | Mundane, antagonistic, political |
| **Creative** | Original, fresh, open-minded, clever, unexpected | Overly literal, trend-chasing, derivative |

---

## Tone (varies by context)

Tone changes based on audience and content. Five reference modes from the Brand Guidelines:

### Encouraging (tutorials, onboarding, support)
- "What will you dream up today with Firefly?"
- "Edit videos that are uniquely you."

### Confident (peer-to-peer, professional, capability-focused)
- "Creativity is everywhere. Now Photoshop is too."
- "Generative AI. This changes everything."

### Enthusiastic (launches, new features)
- "We can't wait to show you what's next."
- "See what new can do."

### Aspirational (creator profiles, customer stories)
- "Five million followers. One big dream."
- "The future is yours to imagine."

### Playful (social, lighter campaigns)
- "Use your words, please."
- "Draw. Paint. Smile."

For sales decks and customer-facing docs the default mode is **Confident**, occasionally shifting to **Aspirational** at the conclusion.

## Mechanical voice rules

### Headlines and subheads
- Sentence case (`Be iconic.` — not `Be Iconic` or `BE ICONIC`)
- End with a period or a question mark
- Track to -20, kerning optical, leading 90–110%
- Keep them short — "Title should be no longer than four lines" (Brand Guidelines)

### Body copy
- Lead with a verb or a noun
- Avoid padding phrases ("in order to", "at the end of the day", "leverage")
- Skip the corporate transitional clauses ("As we move forward into the next phase of our journey...")
- Use contractions when they sound natural ("don't", "we're")
- One idea per sentence; one theme per paragraph

### Pronouns
- "We" for Adobe-the-company
- "You" / "your" for the reader
- Avoid "users" — they're "customers" or "people" or "creators"

### Company name
- In copy: always **Adobe** (no `Inc.`, `Systems`, `Incorporated`)
- In legal entities: `Adobe Inc.` (period after `Inc.`, no comma after `Adobe`)
- Email signatures: "Adobe" or "The team at Adobe"

### Product names
- First mention: full name with "Adobe" prefix — `Adobe Photoshop`, `Adobe Experience Cloud`
- Subsequent: per product guide (usually full name continues; some products drop "Adobe")
- Never abbreviate or acronymize — no `Ps`, `Ai`, `XD` shorthand in customer-facing copy
- Never localize product names

## AI framing — the hardest area to get right

Adobe's position on AI is specific and tightly worded. Every reference to AI in customer materials must align.

### Premise
> Creativity is a human trait. AI delivers productivity gains, but it does not supplant the creative spark or the aesthetic judgment of the user.

### Rules
1. **Don't personify AI** as a creative force or problem-solver.
   - ❌ "The magic of AI"
   - ❌ "AI creates breathtaking work"
   - ✅ "Imagine what you can do with AI"
   - ✅ "Get there faster with AI"

2. **Frame AI as a tool** that enhances efficiency, productivity, iteration, ease.
   - Any claim about creativity or results refers back to the human.

3. **Highlight safeguards** when relevant: Content Credentials, metadata, copyright protections, "commercially safe" designation, attribution. Differentiate Adobe from competitors who don't.

4. **Never frame AI as a replacement** for a creative occupation. AI is not an editor, photographer, designer, writer.
   - ❌ "Skip the photoshoot"
   - ❌ "Create entire scenes from scratch"
   - ✅ "Finish your photoshoot in a flash"
   - ✅ "Every creative journey starts with you. Make the trip easier with AI."

### Drop-in AI lines (safe, on-message)
- "Imagine what you can do with AI."
- "With AI tools, creativity has never been easier or faster."
- "Every creative journey starts with you. Make the trip easier with AI."
- "Finish your photoshoot in a flash."

## Phrases to avoid

Carbohydrate marketing language that creeps into B2B/enterprise decks:
- "best-in-class" / "world-class"
- "the power of"
- "transformational" (overused; reserve for genuine transformation)
- "synergy" / "leverage" / "ideate" / "operationalize"
- "next-generation"
- "robust" / "scalable" / "future-proof"
- "seamless"
- "human-centered" (Adobe says "human" or "people" instead)
- "low-code/no-code" (if used, be specific about what the user actually does)

When tempted to use one, ask: *what would a person actually say at lunch?*

## Headline anti-patterns

Common drift to watch for in deck titles:

| ❌ Drift | ✅ Adobe voice |
|---|---|
| `Driving Enterprise Transformation Through Data-Driven Personalization` | `Personalize at scale.` |
| `The Future of Customer Experience` | `Customer experience, redrawn.` |
| `Empowering Marketers With AI-Powered Tools` | `Marketing, faster with AI.` |
| `Unlocking the Full Potential of Your Creative Workflow` | `Create more. Wait less.` |
| `Adobe Solutions for the Modern Enterprise` | `Adobe at work.` |

The rule: **if it sounds like the headline on a competitor's deck, rewrite it.**

## Inclusive language

- Avoid gendered language ("guys" → "everyone", "team")
- Avoid ableist metaphors ("crazy good" → "really good")
- Avoid stereotyping in imagery and copy
- Pronouns: respect they/them and singular they

---

## Localization

- Product names, cloud names, platform names, taglines used as **branded identity assets**: do NOT localize
- Body copy that supports the message: DO localize (idiomatic translation, not word-for-word)
- Plan names ("photography plan"): may be localized unless product guide says otherwise

---

## When in doubt

Run the line through three filters:

1. **Could a real person say this at lunch?** If not, rewrite.
2. **Would I find this on five competitor decks?** If yes, rewrite.
3. **Does it sound like Adobe — confident, human, creative — or does it sound generic?** If generic, rewrite.

When still unsure, the Brand Guidelines point to `brand@adobe.com` and the Adobe Writing and Style Guide.
